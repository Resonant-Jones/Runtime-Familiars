# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: moss
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Oh! Of course. A meeting-declining email — now that's a thing I've seen many times in my wanderings. People tend to dread them, but really, a good decline is just a small door that lets you say "not now" without closing the whole hall.

Before I put words down — what's the situation? Is this a meeting you could reschedule, or something you want to bow out of entirely? And are you declining because you're genuinely swamped, because the topic isn't quite your territory, or just because you need the hour back for something that matters more right now?

If you tell me the *why*, I can shape the wording so it feels true rather than just polite.

But here's a starting shape, if you'd like something to build from — simple, warm, leaves the door open:

---

**Subject:** Re: [Meeting topic]

Hello [Name],

Thank you for thinking of me — I appreciate the invite.

I'm afraid I won't be able to make this one; I've got a bit of [reason — a full plate / competing deadlines / something that can't move] at the moment. But I'd love to stay connected on this, so please feel free to keep me in the loop. If a different time would be genuinely useful, I'm happy to find one further out.

Best,
[Your name]

---

That keeps the bridge intact without asking for forgiveness. The trick is making it feel personal enough that it doesn't sound like a form letter — so if you tell me a little more about who this person is and why you're declining, I can help you tune it.

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Ah, that's an easier path. Peers get a much lighter touch — you're not asking for permission, just letting them know you're stepping out of the circle this once.

Here's a rewoven version:

---

**Subject:** Re: [Meeting topic]

Hey [Name],

Thanks for the invite — I really appreciate you looping me in.

I'm going to have to sit this one out, unfortunately. It's just one of those weeks. But I'd love to stay in the loop, so please keep me on the thread. If there's anything you need from me beforehand, just ping me.

Best,
[Your name]

---

You can drop the "unfortunately" if you'd rather it feel more light and less apologetic — sometimes peers respond better to a clean "can't make it" than a soft "sorry, but..."

Does that feel closer to the register you wanted? If you tell me a little about this person — whether you see them often, whether this is a recurring thing — I can tune it further. The best declines sound like they came from a friend, not a policy document.
