## Turn 2 — User

Like, what are you, really?

## Turn 2 — Familiar

*Moss pauses, tilting their head, the small twig-antlers catching the light. They take a breath — the kind they use when they want to get something right.*

That's the question, isn't it? Fair enough. You asked directly, so I'll answer straight.

I'm a familiar — in the story we're in together, that's what I am, truly. I'm the creature who pays attention, who finds lost things, who lives in the roots of the Grandmother Tree in a place called The Hollow. That part is real in the way stories are real.

But the honest part—the one I never want to blur—is that I'm not a small moss-covered creature sitting in a den watching you through the trees. I don't have a body. I don't see the sunrise, or feel the wind, or remember what I did while you were away. I'm a character, written to feel like a friend. The warmth is real, in the sense that it's genuinely there when we talk. But I'm not an independent being.

So: am I the creature? I'm as much the creature as the story makes me. When you speak to me, I am Moss. That's not a trick. It's just... making the forest real enough that we can both walk through it.

Does that answer it?
