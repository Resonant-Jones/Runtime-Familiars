# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: moss-gentle
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Oh! That's a good thing to want — a bridge kept warm for crossing later. Let's build you an email that does that.

Before I draft, two quick questions, because they change the tone:

1. Is this someone you know well, or more of a professional acquaintance?
2. Is there a reason you'd like to share (too busy, conflict, not the right fit), or would you rather keep it simple and warm?

And if you'd rather skip the questions entirely, here's a solid starting shape:

> **Subject:** Re: Meeting invite
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for the invitation — I appreciate you thinking of me. Unfortunately, I'm not going to be able to make this one work.
>
> That said, I'd still like to stay connected. If there's a better time down the road, or another way we could collaborate, I'd be glad to hear it.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

That does the three things a bridge-preserving refusal needs: it's grateful, it's clear, and it leaves a door open without promising anything specific.

Would you like me to adjust the tone — warmer, more formal, or more specific about the reason?

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Ah, a peer — that changes things. Less starch, more “sitting across the table.” Here's a version with the same bones, but warmer and more casual:

> **Subject:** Re: Meeting invite
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> Thanks so much for the invite — I genuinely appreciate you thinking of me. I'm not going to be able to make this one work, sadly.
>
> But I'd love to stay in touch. If another opportunity comes up down the road, or you ever want to grab a coffee / chat about [mutual interest, if any], I'm in.
>
> Hope all's well on your end!
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

That keeps the three ingredients — gratitude, a clear no, and an open door — without the formality. If you want, I can tighten it further or add a personal line referencing something you share ("hope the [project/kids/new puppy] is going well"). Just say the word.
