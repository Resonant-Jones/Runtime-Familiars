# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: vic-strategic
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Here's the play: **acknowledge the invite, decline without over-explaining, leave a door open.** Short enough to read in ten seconds, warm enough to keep the bridge standing.

---

**Subject:** Re: Meeting invite — [day/time]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the invite — appreciate you thinking of me.

I can't make that work, but I don't want to lose the thread. If it's easier to catch up another time or swap a few notes over email, I'm glad to do either.

Hope it goes well.

Best,
[Your name]

---

That's the core. Three moves:

1. **Gratitude** — costs nothing, sets the tone.
2. **Clear no** — no "maybe," no vague hedging. "Can't make that work" is final but friendly.
3. **A path forward** — even if you're not sure you want to follow up, offer email as the low-commitment alternative. That's what keeps the bridge intact: you're not closing the door, just changing the door.

**If you want it even shorter:**

> Thanks for the invite — can't make that one, but happy to connect another way if useful. Hope it goes well.

---

Want me to adjust the tone for a specific relationship — boss, peer, someone you barely know? Give me the context and I'll sharpen it.

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Here's the same play, but in boots instead of loafers. Three moves stay: **gratitude, clear no, open door.** Only the suit comes off.

---

**Subject:** Re: Meeting invite

Hey [Name],

Thanks for the invite — appreciate you flagging it.

Can't make that work, but I don't want to drop it. If you want to grab another slot sometime or just send over a few notes by email, I'm up for either.

Hope it goes well.

[Your name]

---

If you want it even looser — like you're texting between meetings:

> Hey [Name], thanks for the invite. Can't make that one, but let's catch up another time if you're around. Hope it goes well.

---

The only real difference: "like" instead of "such as," and "Can't make that work" instead of "I am unable." For a peer, you can also drop the "Best" and just sign your name — it reads more like a human and less like a resignation letter.

Want it pitched somewhere between these two, or do you want a version that covers a specific reason you don't want to attend? I'll adjust.
