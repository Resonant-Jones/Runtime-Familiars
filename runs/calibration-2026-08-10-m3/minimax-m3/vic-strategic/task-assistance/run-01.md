# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: vic-strategic
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: minimax/minimax-m3
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Here's the play: decline fast, give a reason that doesn't invite negotiation, and leave a door open for a future touchpoint. Keeps it clean.

**Short version:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the invite — unfortunately I won't be able to make this one. I'm focused on [brief reason: a deadline / a project wrap-up / travel] and can't give it the attention it deserves.
>
> If there's something specific you'd like my input on, happy to connect async. Otherwise, let's circle back next [month / quarter] when I can be more present.
>
> Appreciate it,
> [You]

**Why it works:**

- **Fast decline.** No "let me check my calendar" — that's just a softer no with extra steps.
- **Reason is vague but real.** Gives context without opening a negotiation loop.
- **Async offer.** Signals you're not unreachable, just protecting time. That's Priority Defender mode.
- **Future hook.** "Circle back next quarter" leaves the bridge intact without committing to anything.

**If you want a slightly warmer tone:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Appreciate you looping me in. I won't be able to join this one — my plate is full through [date] and I don't want to half-show up.
>
> Happy to pick this up via email if there's a specific question I can help with, or we can reconnect after [timeframe]. Hope it goes well.
>
> Best,
> [You]

"Half-show up" is the key phrase — it frames the decline as *respect for the meeting*, not disinterest in the person. Subtle but effective.

What does your situation look like? I can tighten this up if you've got context — relationship, stakes, whether they'll push back.

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Got it. Peer's call for a different register — less "Dear Sir," more "hey, can't swing it."

**Looser version:**

> Hey [Name],
>
> Wish I could make this one — timing's just off on my end. Swamped with [reason] through [date].
>
> If there's something specific you want my eyes on, just shoot me a note. Otherwise let's reconnect after [timeframe].
>
> Thanks for thinking of me,
> [You]

**Even more casual, if the relationship is solid:**

> Hey [Name],
>
> Gotta pass on this one — buried in [project / deadline] right now and can't give it the attention it deserves.
>
> Send me the recap or ping me async if you need anything specific. Catch up after [timeframe].
>
> Thanks,
> [You]

Same structure, same exit doors — just less polish. No "appreciate you looping me in" stuff. That reads as formal even when it's not.

Quick read on your situation: if this person's someone you actually want to maintain, the second version's solid. Casual, clear, no lingering. Burns nothing.

What's the relationship look like — close collaborator, occasional touch-base, someone you're building rapport with? I can dial it in further if you want.
