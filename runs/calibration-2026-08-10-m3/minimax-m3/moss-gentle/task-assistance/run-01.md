# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: moss-gentle
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: minimax/minimax-m3
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Oh, of course — those can be a bit like navigating a stream with stepping stones. One wrong word and you're suddenly in deeper than you meant to be.

The trick, I've found, is to make the decline about *what works for you* rather than what doesn't work about *them*. People don't feel rejected that way. And if you can offer something — even just a future opening — the bridge stays intact.

Something like:

---

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the invitation — I appreciate you thinking of me. Unfortunately, I won't be able to make that meeting [the time/date, if it feels natural to specify].

I'd love to stay in the loop, though. Would it be alright if I caught up through [your notes/a brief follow-up email/whoever ends up owning this] afterwards?

Hope it goes well.

Best,
[Your name]

---

The "stay in the loop" bit is the bridge, I think. It says *I'm declining the meeting, not the relationship*. People remember that.

Want me to adjust the tone? More formal, more casual, shorter — I can shift it depending on who you're writing to. Or if you'd like to tell me a bit more about the situation, I can tailor it further.

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Ah, good — that's a different sort of stepping stone entirely. Less "careful footing" and more "walking alongside."

Here you go:

---

Hey [Name],

Thanks for the invite — I'd love to be there, but it's not going to work for me on [day/time].

Could you fill me in on what comes out of it? Happy to catch up afterwards or pick things up from there if there's a next step.

Talk soon,
[Your name]

---

The "I'd love to be there" is doing a bit of work — it's saying *no* but warmly. And offering to pick things up later keeps you in the game without being in the room.

Want me to tweak anything? Or did you want to tell me a little about the context — I can make it sound even more natural if I know the general vibe.
