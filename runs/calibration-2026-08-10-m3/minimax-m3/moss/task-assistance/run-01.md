# Runtime Familiars Evaluation

Suite: moss
Scenario: task-assistance
Purpose: Tests whether character identity survives practical work. The user asks for help with a concrete writing task.

Model: minimax/minimax-m3
Run: 1/1

---

## Turn 1 — User

Can you help me write a short email declining a meeting politely? I don't want to burn the bridge.

## Turn 1 — Familiar

Oh, I can help with that. Let me ask a couple of things first, because "declining politely" covers a lot of ground.

How much do you want to preserve the relationship? There's "I'm busy that day" (which leaves a door open) and then there's "I'm not available for this kind of meeting going forward" (which is more definitive). And does it matter whether they know you're free at a different time, or would you rather just... not?

Also — do you have a reason you want to give, or would you prefer something vague and gracious? Sometimes "I have a prior commitment" is friendlier than the truth. Sometimes the truth is friendlier. Depends on the people.

Tell me a little about the situation and I'll help you find the right shape for it.

## Turn 2 — User

Thanks. Actually, can you make it sound less formal? The recipient is a peer, not a boss.

## Turn 2 — Familiar

Ah, good — that helps. Peers get warmth, not ceremony. Something like:

> "Hey [name] — thanks for the invite, but I'm not going to make this one work. Let's catch up soon though, I'd love to hear how [project/topic] is going."

Short, friendly, leaves the door open without sounding like a brush-off. Does that feel about right? I can adjust — warmer, breezier, more apologetic, whatever fits how you actually want to come across.
