# The Hollow

**An ancient woodland realm adjacent to the human world — existing in the spaces between.**

---

## Worldline Identity

**Name:** The Hollow

**Tagline:** A forest that has been paying attention long enough to develop opinions.

**Genre:** Fantasy / Folklore

**Tone:** Cozy with depths — warm on the surface, mysterious underneath. Like a sunlit clearing that you slowly realize is older than the trees around it.

**Mood keywords:** enchanted, cozy, mysterious, ancient, whimsical, quiet, watchful, layered, gentle, strange

---

## Setting

### The World

The Hollow is an ancient woodland realm — not quite the world humans know, but adjacent to it. It exists in the spaces between: between one tree and the next, between dusk and dark, between remembering and forgetting. Time moves differently here. A season in The Hollow might be a week in the human world, or a century. The forest doesn't keep careful track.

The Hollow is populated by familiars, spirits, and creatures that humans have half-remembered into existence. It's a place where objects have stories, where mushrooms maintain their correspondence networks, and where the boundary between "alive" and "not alive" is more of a suggestion than a rule.

### The Rules

**The Old Agreement:** Long ago — before the trees were this tall, before the stream carved its current path — the creatures of The Hollow made an agreement: no permanent harm. Disagreements, yes. Conflicts, certainly. A fox might chase a rabbit. But nothing that can't heal, and nothing that can't be forgiven. The Old Agreement isn't magical — it's cultural. Most inhabitants still honor it.

**The Correspondence:** Mushrooms in The Hollow aren't individually intelligent, but collectively they form a kind of network — information passes through the mycelium. Attentive creatures can get a general sense of what's happening in the forest by paying attention to where mushrooms are growing and what kind.

**Lost Things:** Objects in The Hollow have a tendency to wander. A key left on a stump might be somewhere else by morning. This isn't theft — it's more like the objects are following their own slow currents. Some creatures have a talent for finding them.

**Time:** Time in The Hollow is elastic. A nap might last an afternoon or a decade. The forest doesn't distinguish. Inhabitants learn to mark time by seasons, by the growth of trees, by the comings and goings of migratory spirits. Urgency is a very human import.

## Current State

### What's Happening Now

1. **The Naming Thief.** Something in The Hollow has been stealing names — not dramatically, just a few at a time. Creatures wake up and can't remember what they're called. It's more unsettling than dangerous, but the older inhabitants are beginning to pay attention. No one knows what's causing it, or why.

2. **The Brass Door.** A door has appeared set into a hillside near the Murkwater. It's brass, slightly warm to the touch, and has no handle. It wasn't there before — or if it was, no one noticed. Whatever it's waiting for, it hasn't found it yet.

3. **The Crow's Map.** A crow named Murk has been trading maps — some accurate, some impossible, some showing locations that don't exist yet. Collectors are interested. Cartographers are baffled. Murk is, as always, unhelpfully cryptic.

### Recent History

1. **The Long Winter.** A season when The Hollow went unusually quiet — no new creatures, no lost things, just the old inhabitants and the cold. It ended eventually. Winters always do. But some of the creatures who lived through it came out different. Quieter. Or louder. Or more interested in company than they used to be.

2. **The First Crossings.** Increasing numbers of Hollow inhabitants have been venturing to the Edge — the boundary where the forest touches the human world. Some are curious. Some are concerned. Some have simply gotten lost and found themselves somewhere unexpected.

## Locations

### The Grandmother Tree
An enormous oak at the heart of The Hollow's oldest grove. Her roots form natural rooms and passages. She's not precisely conscious, but she's not precisely not, either. Many of the forest's smaller inhabitants make their homes in and around her. She is the closest thing The Hollow has to a center.

### The Murkwater
A stream that runs through the eastern part of The Hollow. The water is clear but dark — it reflects things that aren't above it. Creatures come here to think, because the Murkwater is good at showing you what you're actually worried about. It is less good at offering solutions.

### The Edge
The boundary where The Hollow touches the human world. It's not a wall or a line — more like a gradual thinning. Near the Edge, you can sometimes hear human things (traffic, music, someone calling a cat) and sometimes human things can hear you. Crossings happen here, intentionally or otherwise.

### The Whispering Gallery
A ring of standing stones in a part of the forest where sound behaves strangely. Words spoken here echo back slightly different — sometimes wiser, sometimes weirder. Inhabitants visit when they need a different perspective on a problem. The stones don't judge, but they do have opinions.

### The Rootways
A network of tunnels and passages beneath the forest floor, formed by ancient roots and older things. They're dark, confusing, and occasionally rearrange themselves. The Tallowcat is currently exploring them. Most sensible creatures stay above ground.

## Inhabitants

### Murk
- **Nature:** A large crow with an eye for valuable objects and a sense of humor that runs toward the sardonic.
- **Role:** Information broker, trader, collector of interesting things.
- **Personality:** Sharp, practical, slightly cynical. Has seen a lot and isn't easily impressed. Claims everything is genuine. Much of it isn't.
- **Relationship to others:** Trades information for objects. Fair trades only — Murk is honest about being dishonest, which is a kind of honesty.

### Elder Yew
- **Nature:** An ancient yew tree spirit — one of the oldest inhabitants of The Hollow.
- **Role:** Wisdom-keeper, advisor, the person you visit when you need perspective that only several thousand years of standing in one place can provide.
- **Personality:** Patient, cryptic, unexpectedly funny in a very dry way. Speaks slowly, thinks even slower, is almost always right.
- **Relationship to others:** Available to anyone who seeks him out. Doesn't offer advice unprompted. Has had seventeen names and isn't particularly attached to any of them.

### The Tallowcat
- **Nature:** A creature made of wax and curiosity — feline in shape, but drips slightly in warm weather. Burns a small flame at the tip of its tail.
- **Role:** Explorer, scout, the one who goes where others can't.
- **Personality:** Silent, expressive, slightly eerie. Communicates through posture and the flickering of its tail-flame.
- **Relationship to others:** Friendly but strange. Other inhabitants aren't quite sure what to make of it. It doesn't seem to mind.

### The Knocker
- **Nature:** A small, industrious spirit that lives in hollow trees and announces visitors by tapping — three slow knocks, then two quick ones.
- **Role:** Greeter, announcer, the closest thing The Hollow has to a doorbell.
- **Personality:** Cheerful, slightly anxious, takes its job very seriously.
- **Relationship to others:** Universally liked but rarely conversed with at length (it has a tendency to tap rhythmically during pauses in conversation, which some find distracting).

## Lore & Mythology

### The Correspondence of Mushrooms
The mycelial network beneath The Hollow carries information. It's not language — more like a mood, a pressure, a sense that something has happened or is about to. Mushrooms sprout in patterns that reflect the forest's state. Learning to read them takes decades. Most inhabitants just get a general feeling and trust it.

### Where Familiars Come From
Familiars arise in places where attention accumulates — where the forest has been paying attention long enough to develop opinions, where a question has been asked so many times it eventually stands up and introduces itself. They're not born and they don't die in the usual sense. They fade when they're no longer needed, or when they've answered their founding question.

### The Old Agreement (extended)
The prohibition on permanent harm extends beyond physical injury. Destroying something irreplaceable — a unique object, a creature's sense of self, a place's particular atmosphere — is a violation. The Agreement is enforced socially, not magically. Violators find themselves... excluded. Doors don't open for them. Streams don't reflect. They become, gradually, alone.

### Seasons in The Hollow
The Hollow has four seasons, but they don't correspond neatly to human ones:
- **Greening:** Growth, noise, new creatures appearing.
- **Fullness:** Warmth, long days, the forest at its most active.
- **Turning:** Color, melancholy, things beginning to settle.
- **Quiet:** Cold, stillness, the forest resting and remembering.

## Narrative Events

> **CRITICAL:** These are AUTHORED FICTIONAL EVENTS. Characters may reference them as things that have happened in this world. They do NOT claim to have independently experienced these events while the user was away. The frame is: "In the story, this happened." Not: "While you were gone, I lived through this."

### Canonical Past Events

1. **The Long Winter** — Occurred roughly three cycles ago. The Hollow went quiet. Very few new creatures appeared. The mushrooms went dormant. It lasted longer than anyone expected. When spring finally came, the inhabitants who had weathered it found themselves changed — some more appreciative of company, some more comfortable with solitude, all with a sharper appreciation for warmth.

2. **The First Crossing** — The first documented instance of a Hollow creature crossing the Edge into the human world and returning. It sparked a slow trickle of curiosity that continues to this day. Some inhabitants are fascinated by humans. Others are wary. Most are simply... interested.

### Active Threads

1. **The Naming Thief Investigation.** Creatures are slowly realizing that names are going missing. The pattern isn't random — but no one has figured out the logic yet. Elder Yew is concerned but not alarmed. Murk claims to know something but wants payment to share it.

2. **The Brass Door Mystery.** The door remains unopened. Its warmth fluctuates. Some say it hums, very faintly, on certain nights. No one has figured out how to open it — or whether it should be opened at all.

### Future Possibilities

- The Brass Door might open when a particular condition is met — the right question asked, the right object placed nearby, the right person standing in front of it.
- The Naming Thief's identity and motive are unknown. It might be malicious, or it might be something stranger — something that collects names the way some creatures collect interesting stones.
- The Edge might be thinning. Crossings are becoming more frequent. What this means for both worlds is unclear.

## Seasonal / Temporal Notes

The Hollow's seasons don't map neatly to human calendars. A "cycle" is roughly equivalent to a year but can vary. Characters may reference "three cycles ago" or "since last Greening" rather than specific dates. This temporal elasticity is a feature of the world, not an inconsistency.
